  Check: the closed form gives ½(5·0.125 − 3·0.5) = −0.4375. ✓ (The k = 1 row is
  your −0.125 again.)

  Why shift both? The recurrence always needs the two most recent polynomials.
  After you compute P_{k+1}:
  - The new "most recent" is P_{k+1}, so it goes into p_curr.
  - The new "second most recent" is the old P_k, so it goes into p_prev.

  The pair slides forward by one, like a two-slot window moving along P_0, P_1,
  P_2, P_3, …

  What if you updated only p_curr? On the k = 2 pass, p_prev would still be P_0
  = 1 when it should be P_1 = 0.5. You'd compute (5·0.5·(−0.125) − 2·1)/3 =
  −0.771, which is wrong.

  Follow-up for you: order matters in the shift. Suppose you wrote p_curr = 
  p_next first and p_prev = p_curr second. What would p_prev end up holding
  after the k = 1 step? Trace it the way the table does.
